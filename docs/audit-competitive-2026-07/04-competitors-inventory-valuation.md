# Competitive Landscape: Home Inventory & Personal-Property Valuation

*Six-agent audit & competitive session, July 2026. See 00-synthesis.md. All claims cited inline; caveats in §4.*

---

## CLUSTER A — Home Inventory Apps

- **Sortly** — pivoted to B2B (free ~100 items; $49–299/mo). Photos/folders/QR, insurance PDFs. No stories, beneficiaries, valuation, senior focus. ([pricing](https://www.sortly.com/pricing/))
- **Encircle** — restoration/claims pros; B2B quotes; free consumer inventory tier marketed for "insurance or estate planning." No stories/wishes/senior UX. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/encircle/id604527488))
- **HomeZada** — whole-home suite; free / ~$99/yr Premium / $189 Deluxe (verified). AI photo-to-inventory; explicitly markets **assign items to heirs + "print an estate planning inventory for your estate attorney."** Third parties call it "complex to use." No voice/story, no scam protection, no ranges-with-comps. ([home inventory page](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/home-inventory), [pricing](https://www.homezada.com/homeowners/pricing))
- **Itemtopia** — family/collector inventory; free 20 items; ~$39/yr. No stories/beneficiaries/valuation. ([itemtopia.com](https://www.itemtopia.com/))
- **Under My Roof** — Apple-only, $34.99/yr; voice-activated Quick Entry (voice for *data entry speed*, not story capture), 3D room scan, coverage checks. No wishes/valuation/senior framing. ([undermyroof.app](https://undermyroof.app/))
- **Centriq** — **shut down Jan 31, 2026; all user data deleted.** The category's data-custody cautionary tale — an argument for Keepsake's on-device/exportable posture. ([Dib](https://dib.io/blog/centriq-shutting-down-alternative))
- **Nest Egg** — iOS barcode inventory; barcode auto-fill useless for heirlooms (no UPCs). Minimal maintenance (inferred).
- **HomyScan / Everspruce / NAIC app** — insurance/moving-frame utilities; no legacy layer.
- **SaveOr** — *most Keepsake-adjacent in Cluster A*: AI item ID + estimated values; markets estate planning, probate, downsizing, "senior transitions"; **heir assignment, sentimental notes, Personal Property Memorandum export**; free family collaboration; "Showroom" for selling/estate sales. Weaknesses: aimed at the family/executor doing the transition, not the senior owner; no voice-first story; AI point values not kind ranges; much of the category's "best apps 2026" SEO is SaveOr's own blog. ([saveor.com](https://www.saveor.com/), [estate-sales page](https://www.saveor.com/estate-sales))

## CLUSTER B — Valuation / Appraisal

- **WorthPoint** — ~1B sold records; $29.99/mo / $249.99/yr; just launched **Vault** ("catalog, value, protect," Curator from $149.99/yr) — a live move toward inventory. Collector/reseller UX; no senior/estate/story layer. ([worthpoint.com](https://www.worthpoint.com/), [Vault PR](https://www.worthpoint.com/articles/news/worthpoint-launches-redesigned-vault-giving-collectors-a-smarter-way-to-catalog-value-and-protect-what-they-own))
- **Mearto** — online specialist appraisals $20–69/item, 24–48h; Trustpilot complaints incl. suspected AI-pasted answers. Transactional; no inventory/legacy/senior link. ([mearto.com](https://www.mearto.com/), [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/mearto.com))
- **ValueMyStuff** — ex-Sotheby's/Christie's specialists $10–28/item; 4.6 Trustpilot but "insultingly low" complaints; **valuations, not USPAP appraisals** — not valid for IRS/estate/insurance, not prominently disclosed. ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/valuemystuff.com))
- **Heritage / LiveAuctioneers / Invaluable archives** — free sold-price data built for the trade; Heritage's "free evaluation" is a **consignment-acquisition funnel** — the conflict of interest Keepsake's "never buys what they appraise" rule addresses. ([ha.com archives](https://www.ha.com/information/about-auction-archives.s))
- **eBay Terapeak** — best free mass-market comps (3 years true sold prices); requires seller account + reseller literacy; hopeless UX for a 78-year-old. A plausible data substrate for "what it sells for today." ([eBay help](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/terapeak-research?id=4853))
- **JustAnswer** — chat-an-expert; ~$5 join + ~$30/mo auto-renew; **FTC sued Jan 2026 over dark-pattern subscription enrollment** ($28–125/mo without clear consent) — seniors are the demographic most burned. Keepsake's no-dark-patterns, buy-once plan is the direct counter-position. ([ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/online/justanswer_com.html?page=4))
- **AI "what's it worth" apps** (Curio, Underpriced ~$12/mo, PriceSnap, clones) — ID accuracy 69–92% by category but **value accuracy only 44–68%** (single non-neutral source); confident single numbers — the opposite of range-with-receipts. None senior-first or estate-linked. ([accuracy study](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/ai-antique-appraisal-accuracy/))
- **Artifcts** (straddler; profiled in 03/05) — Arti Lite $45/yr, Unlimited $119/yr (App Store IAP figures); concierge onboarding; AgeTech positioning; Heritage Auctions valuation partner (conflicted); users call pricing "crazy high for what you get" (App Store review).
- **Appraiser societies (ISA/ASA/AAA)** — free find-an-appraiser directories, but bare zip-code searches: no triage, no price transparency, no hand-holding. ([ISA](https://www.isa-appraisers.org/find-an-appraiser), [ASA](https://www.appraisers.org/find-an-appraiser), [AAA](https://www.appraisersassociation.org/find-an-appraiser))

## 2. Pivot threats, ranked — and defenses

1. **Artifcts (~70% there).** Lacks Keepsake's trust architecture: buy-once vs resented subscription; conflict-free directory vs Heritage consignment funnel; scam shield; explainable ranges; emergency guide; family roles. **Defense:** make "no subscription, no conflicted valuations, no dark patterns" the loud brand promise; ship scam-shield and appraiser-preflight as wedges Artifcts structurally can't copy while partnered with an auction house.
2. **SaveOr.** Executor/liquidation center of gravity ("Showroom"). **Defense:** own the senior-as-protagonist (voice stories, kind-decline, face-saving scripts) and the gift motion; SaveOr's liquidation incentive is the honesty gap to exploit.
3. **WorthPoint.** Vault shows intent; brand/price/UX are collector-grade. Better as a **data supplier** than competitor.
4. **HomeZada** — a senior-first pivot fights its whole product surface.
5. **Everplans/vaults** — right audience, wrong object model; would build photo/valuation from zero.

Structural defenses: (a) $129 buy-once is nearly impossible for subscription companies to match without self-cannibalizing; (b) the conflict-free accredited-appraiser network is a two-sided asset that takes time; (c) on-device/exportable custody is newly salient post-Centriq; (d) the JustAnswer FTC suit makes "honest billing for seniors" a marketing moat.

## 3. Specific gaps

- **Inventory + story + wishes + senior UX + valuation-with-kindness: no one has the full combination.** Everyone else has at most two of the five.
- **Scam protection around selling valuables: nobody.** Only editorial advice and government elder-fraud pages exist. "Before You Sell" appears genuinely first-of-kind.
- **Accredited-appraiser routing with transparent pricing: nobody does it well.** Society directories are bare lists; Mearto/VMS sell non-USPAP valuations without prominent disclosure; Heritage funnels to consignment. A transparent tiered preflight exists nowhere else found.
- Secondary: no competitor labels simulated/AI output honestly; no competitor offers a face-saving *decline* framing for low-value items.
