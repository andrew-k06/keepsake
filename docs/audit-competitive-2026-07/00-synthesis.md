# Keepsake — Full Audit + Competitive Positioning Synthesis

*July 2026. Six-agent session: product/UX audit (01), engineering audit (02), estate-organizer landscape (03), inventory/valuation landscape (04), story-capture landscape (05), voice-of-customer + pricing (06). This file is the consolidated read.*

## I. What separates Keepsake (the differentiation write-up)

The market for "what happens to my things" has organized itself into **three camps that don't talk to each other**, and Keepsake is the only product standing in the middle:

1. **Story-only** — StoryWorth, Remento, HereAfter, MyStories, the AI-memoir long tail. Stories attach to *prompts and life chapters*, never to possessions. None touch wishes, values, or documents. Their #1 documented failure is **abandonment**: 52-week programs the parent never finishes, punished with renewal fees.
2. **Thing-only** — home-inventory apps (Sortly, HomeZada, Itemtopia), valuation services (WorthPoint, Mearto, ValueMyStuff), and FairSplit's post-death division bidding. Items and dollar figures with **zero story and zero voice** — and in FairSplit's case, the family fight monetized *after* the owner can no longer speak.
3. **Docs-only** — Everplans, Prisidio, Trustworthy, GoodTrust, Trust & Will. Vaults and legal instruments. The belongings layer is a text field or a "schedule of assets."

**Keepsake's position: the story attached to the thing, plus what happens to the thing.** One record carries the photo, the owner's voice telling why it matters, who they'd love to have it, what it honestly sells for today, and the practical trail (memorandum for the attorney, inventory for the insurer, printed binder for the family). Nobody else produces that record. The closest competitor, **Artifcts**, has the object-story half (audio stories, QR-linked objects, Heritage Auctions valuations) but stops short of structured wishes, the guided path, any protection layer, the gift motion, and buy-once pricing — and its valuation partner is an auction house that profits when you consign, the exact conflict Keepsake's "never buys what they appraise" rule forbids.

Six separators, each evidenced across the research:

1. **The truth architecture.** Keepsake never asserts what it can't verify — owner-attested insurance, wishes-not-bequests, labeled previews, ranges-in-words, an appraisal router that says "you don't need to pay anyone for this one." The category context makes this a weapon: JustAnswer was sued by the FTC (Jan 2026) for dark-pattern subscriptions charged to seniors; StoryWorth runs defensive ads about auto-renewal; Remento charges $50 to export your own stories. Honesty here isn't tone — it's the moat.
2. **Buy-once pricing in a subscription sea.** Every adjacent digital product is $99–288/yr recurring. The only buy-once successes are *paper* (Nokbox: claimed $60M sold at ~$169) — proof the wallet exists — and nobody digital occupies it. Subscription resentment is documented on four separate complaint fronts. $129-once with the subscription only on the child-paid coaching plan is structurally hard for incumbents to copy without cannibalizing themselves.
3. **The two-sided design.** Every competitor picks one user: the motivated self-serve planner (Everplans, Artifcts) or the executor/child (Nokbox, FairSplit, SaveOr, Carefull). Keepsake designs both sides of the actual transaction — the adult child pays, gets scripts and read access; the parent authors, decides, and keeps dignity — with gift onboarding, Together mode, and parent-voiced confirmations.
4. **The protection layer.** "Before You Sell" has **no competitor anywhere** — the research found only editorial advice and FBI warning pages (98% of $26M in gold-courier losses came from victims over 60). Connecting an item's comps to the offer in grandma's hand, with a face-saving doorstep script, is a first-of-kind feature sitting on data Keepsake already holds.
5. **The completion answer.** The story category's defining failure is abandonment. Keepsake's design answers it three ways: the item in hand is the prompt (no 52-week program), voice removes the writing burden, and the Getting Ready path credits work invisibly (derived progress) with "Not today" as a first-class button.
6. **Data custody.** Centriq shut down in January and deleted everyone's data; SafeBeyond gave users 30 days; Remento locks lapsed users out of their own stories. Keepsake is on-device, printable, and (once the export button ships) exportable — "your binder outlives us" is a claim no cloud-vault competitor can make.

**One-line positioning against each neighbor:** StoryWorth writes the memoir but doesn't know the ring exists; HomeZada knows the ring's price but not its story; Everplans knows where the will is but not the will's hardest question; FairSplit referees the fight after Mom is gone; Nokbox is the binder that can't hear her voice; Artifcts catalogs the story but not the wish. Keepsake is the ring, the story in her voice, her wish, its honest value, and the printed page — in one record.

## II. Where their gaps exist and where we capitalize

Ranked by (evidence strength × how directly the existing build answers it):

| # | Gap (evidenced) | Who leaves it open | Capitalize with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Undocumented who-gets-what; sentimental items cause the worst estate fights | Everyone; FairSplit monetizes it post-mortem | Core product + memorandum export. GTM: "the $129 that prevents the $10,000 fight." |
| 2 | Subscription fatigue; no digital buy-once exists | Entire digital field | Lead pricing with "yours forever, no monthly fees"; contrast Remento's $50 PDF ransom explicitly in comparison content |
| 3 | Data lock-in horror stories (Centriq, SafeBeyond, Remento) | All cloud vaults/story apps | Ship JSON export; publish the "your binder outlives us" promise; print is the ultimate escrow |
| 4 | Story abandonment (52-week programs) | StoryWorth, Remento, MyStories | Object-anchored micro-stories, voice-first, derived progress; market "no homework, no deadline" |
| 5 | Senior-targeted selling scams; zero product answers | Carefull (accounts only), AARP content | Before You Sell as the PR/press wedge ("the app that stops the driveway gold buyer") and the AARP conversation-starter |
| 6 | Valuation is either hype-AI point numbers (44–68% accurate) or 2–3-week conflicted appraisals | AI apps, Heritage-funnel, bare ISA/ASA directories | Ranges-in-words + kind decline + transparent conflict-free preflight; potential WorthPoint/Terapeak data supply rather than rivalry |
| 7 | Tech not designed for 50+ (AARP: 64%) | Everplans crashes, vault complexity | The senior-first system + voice; publish the design principles as content marketing |
| 8 | Morbidity blocks the category; the talk goes fine once started (Fidelity/Ameriprise) | Everyone frames as planning/vaults | Gift framing + Getting Ready path + the printed binder as the conversation's prop |
| 9 | 53% of homeowners have no inventory; inventory apps are cold or pro-priced | Sortly et al. | The professional inventory export falls out free; insurer channel per the sales plan |
| 10 | Nobody fuses the gift moment (memoir) with the trigger moment (downsizing/estate) | Remento owns gifts; Artifcts owns pros | The gift SKU that quietly handles the practical part — the fused motion neither can copy easily |

**Threat watch:** Artifcts adding a gift SKU + wishes flow is the realistic fast-follow (they have AgeTech positioning, a new CTO, and the pro channel); SaveOr's executor-framed memorandum export is the mechanical copy; WorthPoint's new Vault shows the data owners drifting toward inventory; Empathy ($162M) is the funded giant if it ever goes direct-to-consumer. Defenses: speed on the wedge features (scam shield, guided path, gift motion), the pricing structure, and the conflict-free/truth brand they'd each have to break their business model to match.

**⚠️ Naming risk (route to a decision):** at least two active companies already operate as "Keepsake" in memory-preservation — keepsakeproject.co (memoir SaaS, publishes attack-SEO against StoryWorth/Remento) and makekeepsake.com. Trademark and SEO collision is near-certain; rename or clear marks before any public launch.

## III. What to improve (consolidated from both internal audits)

**Fix now (bugs & truth violations the new waves introduced):**
1. Truth-sweep round 2: Plan page promises "export" (doesn't exist) and "unlimited rooms" (rooms can't be created); TrendCard points at an appraisal section that's often absent; gift flow doesn't actually enter Together mode; guide steps complete on intent (button press) not act.
2. Offer Check silent no-op (custom item without a value → no verdict, yet step + audit still credited).
3. Correctness cluster: IndexedDB connection churn (`useRef(createRepository())`), celebration-barrage bypass via `completeStep` before `startPath`, the `ownerName === 'Margaret'` wipe-without-confirm sentinel, missing audit lines on emergency-note edits, no save flush on tab close.
4. Demo recoverability: nothing restores Margaret's example binder; seed images are remote Unsplash URLs that break offline demos.

**Build next (product):** item editing + add-photo-later (the biggest dead-end cluster) · room management · JSON export/import · guide return-loop completion + mobile Plan/Guide access · merge the two trusted-contact concepts · search + all-items view · documents/multi-photo capture.

**Build next (engineering):** `navigator.storage.persist()` (iOS evicts IndexedDB after ~7 days idle — silently breaks the core promise) · photos out of the binder blob into separate IDB records (15MB clones per save at 50 items) · multi-tab version guard · migration hardening with a versioned envelope · extract store updaters to `lib/binder.ts` + Vitest (10 tests) + check in the Playwright e2e · lazy-load print/guide routes · PWA manifest · iOS Web Speech auto-restart (pauses cut elders off mid-story).

**Suggested order:** (1) truth-sweep + bug cluster (days) → (2) durability trio: persist()/export/multi-tab (the "cannot be silently lost" theme) → (3) item editing + rooms + demo restore (demo & pilot readiness) → (4) tests + photo-blob split (pilot substrate) → (5) search/documents/PWA.
