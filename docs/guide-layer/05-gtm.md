# Keepsake — GTM Impact Assessment: The Guide Layer

*Guide-layer concept session, July 2026. Builds on docs/review-2026-07/04-sales.md and 05-marketing.md. See 00-synthesis.md for the consolidated concept.*

*Verdict up front: the guide is net-strongly-positive for GTM — but only if it faces the adult child as "find out what's missing" and faces the senior as "we'll walk you through it," never as "get prepared." It is the missing answer to the real competitor (doing nothing) and the honest justification for the Family Plan subscription that trend-watching never was.*

---

## 1. Positioning Shift: strengthens — if the guide is the *how*, not the *what*

**The tension is real but resolvable.** "The story of your things, preserved as a gift" is a noun — a treasure. "The guide that walks Mom through getting prepared" is a verb — a process. Led with, the process frame drags the brand straight into the morbidity trap: *preparedness* is what you do before you die, and estate-avoidance is the defining behavior of this market. Nobody gifts Mom a curriculum about her own mortality.

**Resolution: the guide is not a new promise; it's the answer to the oldest objection — "I keep meaning to, but I don't know where to start."** The locked one-liner stays. The guide gets a sub-line:

> **Keepsake — every treasure in your home has a story. Save both, for the people you love.**
> *And you won't do it alone — Keepsake walks you through it, one step at a time.*

**Message hierarchy, by audience:**

| Audience | Guide's rank | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Senior (user) | **Proof point** — the connective tissue under all three existing pillars | "One step at a time, when you're ready." Never "prepare," "estate," "checklist for the end." The verbs are *walk through, finish the story, so your family always knows what to do* |
| Adult child (buyer) | **Lead hook** — the acquisition message | "You don't know if Mom has any of this handled. Find out in 5 minutes — then Keepsake walks her through the rest." |
| Family Plan (upsell) | **The honest recurring service** | "We keep the whole family on track, year after year" |

**Pillar integration (no fourth pillar needed):** the guide supplies proof to all three existing pillars — story prompts and voice-capture walkthroughs prove *"The story is the treasure"*; the decision modules and family-conversation scripts prove *"No surprises, no squabbles"*; the plain-English papers checklist and "this isn't legal advice" honesty prove *"Yours, private, and in plain English."* And it demolishes the real competitor row in the alternatives table: against "doing nothing," the counter is no longer just "save one story today" — it's "we'll tell you exactly what today's five minutes should be."

**Naming discipline:** never "Estate Prep Guide" or "Preparedness Program." Working name: **the Keepsake Path** (or simply "the Guide"), with module titles framed as gifts and competence — "Tell the First Story," "Who Gets What, and Why," "Where the Papers Live," "The Family Conversation."

---

## 2. Buyer Impact: yes — the guide becomes the hook that sells the binder

The adult child's anxiety is diagnostic, not archival: *"I don't know if Mom has ANY of this handled."* The binder answers "where do her stories live?"; the guide answers "is anything missing?" — which is the question that opens wallets.

### The free "Family Check-Up" (design)

- **Format:** 5 minutes, ~12 questions, no email wall to *take* it (email to save/share results). Four areas:
  1. **The Stories** — "Could you tell the story behind three things she treasures?"
  2. **The Wishes** — "Is who-gets-what written anywhere, or does everyone just *think* they know?"
  3. **The Papers** — "Could you find her will, deed, and insurance policies this afternoon?"
  4. **The Conversation** — "Have you ever actually talked about it — or does it always get postponed?"
- **Results page:** a warm four-area readiness map ("2 of 4 areas need attention") — **never a fear-score**; each gap paired with the exact guide module that closes it and a "most families handle this in one afternoon" reassurance. Tone: truth, dignity, agency.
- **The sibling-share mechanic (the viral unit):** "Send this to your sister — compare answers." Siblings who answer *differently* about who gets the ring have just experienced the product's core problem live. Mismatch is the demo.
- **Funnel:** ad/content → Check-Up → results page → primary CTA **"Give Mom the Guide + Binder — $129, hers forever, no subscription"** (gift flow, physical welcome kit includes the printed first module) → secondary CTA: 6-part email course ("The Talk, week by week") for not-ready-yets, with gap-specific retargeting.
- **Metrics:** Check-Up completion rate, completion→gift conversion, sibling-share rate, gift activation (still the decisive metric), email-course→purchase lag.

This replaces the generic quiz on "The Talk" landing page and becomes the destination for the planned Meta test.

## 3. Channel Effects: the guide feeds every channel — with one guardrail

**Estate attorneys — feeds, decisively.** Attorneys are threatened by *DIY document mills*, not education; they're exhausted by unprepared clients. A guide that never drafts anything but manufactures clients who arrive with an inventory, a beneficiary map, and a list of questions is a referral machine. Cement it structurally:
- **Every module ends with a printable "Questions for your attorney" sheet** — the module's output *is* an attorney agenda. Pair with the memorandum export.
- **Co-branded attorney edition:** the attorney's name and number printed on the questions sheet of the binders they gift.
- **Reverse referral flow (new):** attorneys hand the *free* Check-Up + Module 1 to prospects as their own lead magnet — Keepsake becomes the attorney's client-warming tool, not just the attorney becoming ours.
- Guardrail: the guide teaches *concepts and questions*, never legal conclusions. This is simultaneously the compliance line and the partnership glue.

**Senior communities — the curriculum IS the product.** Upgrade "Workshop-in-a-Box" (one 60-minute session) to **a six-week course: "The Story of Your Things."** Activity directors' scarcest resource is *recurring* programming; a series with printed weekly worksheets justifies raising the site license (~$25/resident/yr). Attendance week over week is the retention proof for the case study.

**Senior move managers (NASMM) — a billable service line.** A light "Keepsake Guide Facilitator" track: move managers deliver the curriculum at their $60–100/hr rates, Keepsake supplies materials and takes the software revenue.

**Financial advisors — the family-meeting tool.** The Conversation module (agenda, scripts, printed summary) is the multigenerational family meeting in a box. Positions Keepsake inside wealth-transfer conversations without touching regulated advice.

**Insurers — a scheduled trigger instead of a hope.** The Papers module includes "check what your insurance actually covers today" — placing the referral moment *inside* the curriculum at a predictable week. (Still gated on the state-licensing memo.)

## 4. Monetization & Packaging

| Tier | Guide content | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Family Check-Up, Module 1 ("Tell the First Story"), the conversation-starters guide, emergency-basics checklist | Education-first trust + lead-gen; genuinely useful with $0 spent — that generosity *is* the trust strategy |
| **Binder ($129 one-time)** | Full guide: all modules, printables, "Questions for your attorney" sheets | The guide justifies holding the $129 price (skip the $99 launch discount): it's now a course + a tool, anchored against Trust & Will at $199–499 and paid estate workshops at $50–150 |
| **Family Plan ($79/yr, child pays)** | **Ongoing coaching:** annual review walkthrough ("it's been a year — new grandchild? sold the china?"), seasonal prompts, family-conversation scheduling, milestone progress visible to the child, new modules as released — plus trends/alerts, demoted from headline to feature | See below |
| **New SKU: Gift Kit ($149)** | Binder + **printed workbook companion** in the physical welcome box | Physical product fits the trust strategy (paper-fallback promise) and the gift motion; the workbook is what Mom unwraps |
| **New SKU: Facilitated Family Session ($199–299, pilot manually)** | 90-minute guided family conversation, delivered by trained facilitators or move-manager partners (Keepsake takes ~20% when partner-delivered) | The Conversation module productized; validates before any tech is built |

**The Family Plan question — yes, and this matters strategically.** "Ongoing coaching" is a far more honest subscription surface than trend-watching. Trends are passive, compliance-fraught, and easy to churn from. What the adult child actually pays $79/yr for is *knowing it's handled and staying handled* — annual reviews, nudges, and visible progress deliver exactly that. Reposition: **"The Family Plan — Keepsake keeps the whole family on track, year after year."** One dignity guardrail: the child-visible progress dashboard must be milestone-level and senior-consented ("Mom chose to share her progress") — coaching, never surveillance. Getting this wrong recreates the fairness-scoreboard mistake at the relationship level.

## 5. Proof & Trust: teaching is the opposite of a scam

**The pattern:** education-first brands win skeptical audiences because scams *rush* and teachers *pace* — this generation's most-trusted institutions are precisely the ones that taught before they asked (AARP is an education organization with products attached; banks' fraud-education programs; the fiduciary-advisor model). A free Check-Up and a genuinely useful free module extend "when you're ready" from copy into behavior — the brand demonstrably does not need you to buy today. The guide also makes the trust kit *bigger*: the printed workbook is more paper-fallback proof, and "this isn't legal advice — bring these questions to your attorney" honesty on every module is the truth-sweep ethic applied to content.

**Claims discipline:**
- **Cannot say:** "Be prepared in 6 weeks" (outcome guarantee), "your affairs will be in order" (implies legal adequacy), "complete estate readiness," anything scoring "readiness" as if certified. FTC treats elder-targeted deception as aggravated.
- **Can say:** "Six short steps. Most families finish in about six weeks — at your own pace." / "Know exactly where you stand in 5 minutes." / "Every step ends with the questions to ask your attorney." Descriptive, paced, verifiable.

**Testimonial shape — the dual-voice family unit:** the child and the parent, together. *"Keepsake walked us through everything — the stories, the papers, the conversation we'd been putting off for three years."* (child) + *"I finally told them why there are only eleven plates."* (parent). The guide gives testimonials a narrative arc (before → walked through → after) that a storage product never had.

## 6. Top 5 GTM Actions if the Guide Ships (ordered)

1. **Ship the free Family Check-Up as the new top of funnel.** Replace the generic quiz on "The Talk" landing page, wire results directly into the gift flow with gap-specific CTAs and the sibling-share mechanic, and point the planned Meta test at it. Decision metric: Check-Up completion → gift purchase.
2. **Lock the dual-frame messaging and sweep the guide's copy.** Keep the master one-liner, add the "walks you through it" sub-line, name the guide, write senior-facing modules in competence/legacy language and child-facing acquisition copy in "find out what's missing" language; apply the claims-discipline rules.
3. **Repackage the tiers.** Full guide into the $129 binder (hold price), rebuild the Family Plan around ongoing coaching + annual review + consented progress (demote trends), launch the $149 Gift Kit with printed workbook for the Mother's/Father's Day motion.
4. **Rebuild the estate-attorney kit around the guide.** "Questions for your attorney" endings, co-branded attorney edition, reverse-referral free Check-Up for their prospects, memorandum-export tie-in — then run the 10-attorney metro pilot with this stronger offer.
5. **Upgrade Workshop-in-a-Box to the six-week community curriculum + facilitator track.** Printed weekly worksheets, raised site-license pricing, NASMM facilitator certification pilot; one community through the full six weeks → the case study that opens the association channel.

**Key risk to carry forward:** the guide doubles the surface area where the app could assert things that aren't true — every checklist item, every "most families finish in six weeks," every module claim now falls under the truth-sweep bar. The guide only compounds trust if its content is held to the same standard as the product's UI copy.
